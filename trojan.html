What Is a Trojan Horse?

A Trojan horse, usually just called a Trojan, is a type of malware that basically tricks someone into thinking they are downloading or opening something completely normal. It could look like a regular program, a file, or even something useful, but once it gets opened, things can go very wrong! The malware can then start doing things the user never agreed to.

How Do Trojans Work?

The interesting thing about Trojans is that they rely on people trusting what they see. They can show up in fake downloads, email attachments, or programs that look legitimate, and once someone opens one, the Trojan can start running in the background without the user knowing what just happened!

01. The Disguise

First, the Trojan pretends to be something harmless, like a normal application or file. The goal is to make someone trust it enough to open it without thinking twice.

02. The Infection

Once the file is opened, the Trojan can start doing what it was designed to do, which could include stealing information or giving an attacker access to the computer.

Common Types of Trojans

There are actually quite a few different types of Trojans, and they don't all do the same thing. Some are focused on stealing information, while others are designed to give attackers control over someone else's computer!

Backdoor Trojan

Creates a hidden way into a computer, allowing an attacker to get access without the user knowing or giving permission.

Banking Trojan

Goes after banking information, passwords, and other financial details that could allow attackers to access someone's money or accounts.

Remote Access Trojan

Allows an attacker to connect to someone else's computer and control it remotely, sometimes without the user even noticing!

Downloader Trojan

Once it gets onto a computer, it can download and install even more malware, basically bringing additional threats onto an already infected device.

How to Stay Protected

Avoiding Trojans mostly comes down to being careful about what you download, click on, and open. Always make sure programs come from websites you trust, and be extra careful with random email attachments or downloads that seem a little suspicious. Just because something looks safe doesn't mean it actually is!
