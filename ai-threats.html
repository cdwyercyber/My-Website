Artificial Intelligence and Cybersecurity

Artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming increasingly common, but unfortunately, it isn't always being used for good! Cybercriminals can use AI to create scams, impersonate people, and find new ways to attack computer systems.

Common AI Threats

AI Phishing

AI can create realistic emails and messages that make phishing scams much harder to recognize.

Deepfakes

AI can generate fake images, videos, and audio that imitate real people. This can make impersonation scams especially convincing!

Prompt Injection

Attackers use specially written instructions to trick an AI system into ignoring its rules or revealing information.

Data Poisoning

Attackers put misleading information into AI training data, potentially causing the system to produce incorrect results.

AI Threat Comparison

These threats might sound similar, but they each work in different ways. Here's a closer look at what attackers are doing and what can happen when these attacks succeed.

Threat How It Works Potential Impact
AI Phishing Creates convincing emails and messages. Stolen passwords, personal information, and financial loss.
Deepfakes Imitates real people using generated images, video, or audio. Impersonation, fraud, and scams.
Prompt Injection Manipulates an AI system using crafted instructions. Exposed information or unintended actions.
Data Poisoning Introduces misleading information into AI training data. Incorrect or manipulated AI results.

Staying Safe

So, what can you do about it? A lot of the same cybersecurity habits still apply! Just keep a few things in mind:

  • Be careful with unexpected messages and suspicious links.
  • Verify someone's identity before sending money or information.
  • Don't automatically trust videos or audio recordings.
  • Use strong passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Avoid sharing sensitive information with untrusted AI tools.
