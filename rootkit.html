What Is a Rootkit?

A rootkit is a type of malware that is designed to hide itself deep inside a computer system, making it extremely difficult to detect and remove. What makes rootkits so dangerous is that they can give attackers long-term access to a device while hiding their activity from the user and even some security programs. Basically, a rootkit can allow someone to secretly control parts of a system without anyone realizing it!

Why Are Rootkits So Dangerous?

Rootkits are especially concerning because they don't just try to infect a computer, they also try to hide the evidence that they are even there! Some can operate with extremely high system privileges, giving attackers powerful access to a device. This can make it much harder to figure out what has been compromised and what the attacker might have already done.

What Can a Rootkit Do?

Depending on how it is designed, a rootkit can do quite a bit of damage while staying hidden. Some examples of what attackers may use them for include:

  • Hiding malicious files and running processes.
  • Giving attackers hidden access to a computer.
  • Concealing other malware already on the system.
  • Interfering with security software and system tools.

Can Rootkits Be Removed?

Unfortunately, removing a rootkit isn't always as simple as running an antivirus scan. Since some rootkits can hide from normal security tools, they may require specialized detection methods or scanning the system from a trusted environment outside the infected operating system. In serious cases, completely reinstalling the operating system may be necessary!
